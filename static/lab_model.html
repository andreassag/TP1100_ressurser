Physiological vs. Benchtop Model Parameter Mapping

Physiological Parameter Physical Benchtop Mechanism Digital Twin Slider (Model 1 & 2) Key Student Observation & Equation
Tidal Volume ($V_T$) Graduated 100 mL Syringe Piston stroke $(\Delta V)$ Tidal Volume (mL) (150–1000 mL) Volume of air displaced per stroke. Measured in mL directly from syringe barrel markings.
Breathing Rate ($RR$) Metronome cadence / timed piston strokes per minute Respiratory Rate (6–35 breaths/min) Minute Ventilation $\dot{V}_E = V_T \times RR$. Dictates cycle period $T = 60/RR$.
Anatomical Dead Space ($V_D$) Modular clear vinyl tracheal tubes (5 mL short vs. 25 mL extension) + lightweight pith ball tracer Dead Space VD (mL) (50–350 mL) Alveolar Ventilation $\dot{V}_A = (V_T - V_D) \times RR$.
When $V_T \le V_D$, tracer bead oscillates inside the tube without fresh air ever reaching balloons ($\dot{V}_A = 0$, 0% efficiency!).
Airway Resistance ($R_{aw}$) IV Roller Clamps or screw pinch valves on bronchial tubes Airway Resistance Raw (1–20 cmH2O·s/L) Ohm's Law: $\Delta P = \dot{V} \times R_{aw}$.
Narrowing clamps increases hand resistance, widens P–V loop, creates phase lag, and produces auto-PEEP air trapping at high $RR$.
Lung Compliance ($C_L$) Swappable balloons: standard latex (normal) vs. banded/thickened (fibrosis) vs. nitrile bag (emphysema) Lung Compliance CL (0.04–0.35 L/cmH2O) Static Compliance $C_L = \Delta V / \Delta P_{pl}$.
Fibrotic balloons require huge negative pressure swings on the manometer to expand; emphysematous balloons expand easily but fail to recoil.
Intrapleural Pressure ($P_{pl}$) U-Tube Water Manometer with colored water against metric ruler ($\Delta h$) Intrapleural Pressure (cmH2O readout & P–V graph) Direct physical hydrostatic pressure: $1\text{ cmH}_2\text{O} = 1\text{ cm}$ fluid height difference.
Resting FRC baseline $-4$ to $-5\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$, dropping to $-8$ to $-12\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$ during inspiration.
Pneumothorax 3-way stopcock valve opening thoracic chamber to room air Simulated seal breach Opening stopcock allows air to rush into pleural space: $P_{pl} \to 0\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$, transpulmonary pressure collapses balloons immediately.

Classroom Lab Rig

Bill of Materials (BOM) & Assembly Guide

Designed for high educational fidelity at ultra-low cost ($< $35 total per student workstation). All parts are readily available from hardware stores, medical surplus, or educational science suppliers.

Item # Component Specification Est. Cost Role & Tips
1 Thoracic Chamber Clear rigid acrylic jar or heavy-duty wide-mouth PET canister (2.5–3.0 L) with screw lid and rubber gasket $8.00 Must hold airtight seal up to $\pm 30\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$. Drill 3 top holes: 1 for tracheal inlet, 1 for manometer port, 1 for stopcock.
2 Diaphragm Piston 100 mL catheter-tip plastic syringe (graduated) $3.50 Inserted through a rubber grommet at the bottom or lower side of the chamber. Lubricate plunger with a drop of mineral oil for smooth motion.
3 Carina Y-Connector Plastic barbed Y-connector (3/8" or 1/2" ID) $1.50 Splits the single tracheal tube into left and right mainstem bronchial branches.
4 Conducting Airway Tubing Clear food-grade vinyl tubing (3/8" ID), 1 meter total $3.00 Cut into short segment (trachea, 5 cm) and long swappable segment (snorkel extension, 25 cm) to vary dead space volume.
5 Airway Resistance Clamps 2x IV Roller Clamps or screw-type laboratory pinch clamps $2.00 Placed over the left and right bronchial vinyl tubing inside the chamber or accessible via external ports.
6 Alveolar Balloons (Compliance Set) Pack of 9" latex party balloons + small elastic O-rings + nitrile finger cots $3.00 • Normal: 1 single balloon per side.
• Fibrosis: 2 nested balloons or 3 rubber bands around balloon.
• Emphysema: Pre-stretched thin nitrile bag or repeatedly inflated balloon.
7 U-Tube Water Manometer 1 meter of clear 1/4" ID vinyl tubing bent into U-shape, mounted on rigid backing with cm ruler, filled with dyed water $4.00 Zero-point marked at liquid equilibrium level. Each cm of fluid deflection equals $1\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$.
8 Pneumothorax Valve Plastic 3-way luer-lock stopcock with barbed adapter $2.00 Threaded through lid. Closed = intact pleural cavity; Open = open pneumothorax.

Airtight Assembly Checklist: Use silicone sealant or hot glue around all lid feed-throughs. To test seal integrity: close stopcock, push syringe in by 30 mL, hold for 10 seconds. The water manometer height must remain completely static without drift.

Classroom Lab Exercises

4 Hands-On Student Lab Protocols

Each protocol has students record physical measurements on the benchtop rig, then enter the values into the digital twin to observe the resulting alveolar gas exchange and oxygenation efficiency.

🧪 Experiment 1: Dead Space & Breathing Strategy

Core Concept: Why rapid shallow breathing causes acute hypoxemia even when minute ventilation is normal.

V̇_A = (V_T - V_D) × RR   Efficiency = V̇_A / V̇_E
  1. Keep total minute ventilation constant at $\dot{V}_E = 6000\text{ mL/min}$.
  2. Condition A (Normal Deep Breathing): Set syringe stroke $V_T = 500\text{ mL}$ (scaled to 50 mL on rig) at $RR = 12\text{ min}^{-1}$. Observe the foam tracer bead travel fully into the carina. Alveolar ventilation is $4.2\text{ L/min}$ (70% efficiency).
  3. Condition B (Rapid Shallow Panting): Switch to $V_T = 200\text{ mL}$ (20 mL on rig) at $RR = 30\text{ min}^{-1}$. Notice the bead oscillates within the upper dead space tube without ever reaching the balloons! Alveolar ventilation plummets to $1.5\text{ L/min}$ (25% efficiency).
  4. Condition C (Snorkel Extension): Attach the 25 cm extension tube ($V_D = 300\text{ mL}$). At $V_T = 250\text{ mL}$, alveolar ventilation is strictly 0 mL/min!
Check in Digital Twin: Click the "Shallow Rapid (Dead Space Trap)" preset in Model 1. Note the red efficiency warning and link to Model 2 to see the resulting arterial desaturation.

📊 Experiment 2: Lung Compliance & Elastic Work

Core Concept: How tissue stiffness changes intrapleural pressure swings and elastic Work of Breathing.

C_L = ΔV / ΔP_pl   W_elastic = 0.5 × (V_T² / C_L)
  1. Record baseline resting manometer level (FRC, approx $-4\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$).
  2. Condition A (Normal Compliance): Pull syringe piston by $\Delta V = 50\text{ mL}$. Record the change in water manometer height $\Delta P_{pl}$. Calculate $C_L = 50\text{ mL} / \Delta P$.
  3. Condition B (Pulmonary Fibrosis): Swap standard balloon for double-layered balloon with rubber bands. Pull syringe by the same $\Delta V = 50\text{ mL}$. Notice you must pull much harder, and the manometer swings to a deeply negative pressure ($\Delta P_{pl}$ more than doubles!). Calculate the elevated Work of Breathing.
  4. Condition C (Emphysema): Install thin pre-stretched balloon. Notice it inflates with minimal $\Delta P_{pl}$, but fails to deflate passively when syringe is released.
Check in Digital Twin: Select "Pulmonary Fibrosis" preset in Model 1. Notice the steeper static compliance line and the huge increase in required negative intrapleural pressure.

💨 Experiment 3: Airway Resistance & Dynamic Flow Lag

Core Concept: Airway narrowing, flow phase lag, peak resistive pressure, and dynamic hyperinflation (auto-PEEP).

ΔP_total = (ΔV / C_L) + R_aw × V̇
  1. Start with bronchial roller clamps fully open ($R_{aw} \approx 2\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}\cdot\text{s/L}$).
  2. Pull and push syringe at $RR = 15\text{ min}^{-1}$. Note that balloon expansion is virtually instantaneous with piston movement.
  3. Tighten the roller clamps to 75% occlusion (simulating asthma / severe bronchospasm).
  4. Repeat piston strokes: observe the noticeable phase lag—the syringe finishes pulling while balloons are still slowly expanding.
  5. Increase rate to $RR = 25\text{ min}^{-1}$: notice balloons do not have enough expiratory time to empty before the next stroke begins (dynamic air trapping / auto-PEEP).
  6. Clamp only the left bronchus: observe asymmetric ventilation (the unobstructed right lung gets virtually all the tidal volume).
Check in Digital Twin: Select "Asthma / High Resistance" preset. Watch the P–V hysteresis loop widen dramatically, reflecting high resistive work.

⚠️ Experiment 4: Pleural Seal & Pneumothorax

Core Concept: Transpulmonary pressure gradient and chest wall mechanics.

P_tp = P_alv - P_pl   If P_pl = P_atm ⇒ P_tp ≤ 0
  1. While cycling the syringe and observing healthy balloon inflation, quickly open the 3-way stopcock on the chamber lid.
  2. Observe what happens: room air rushes into the chamber through the valve; the water manometer drops immediately to $0\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$.
  3. Continue cycling the syringe piston: notice the balloons do not inflate at all! Instead, air merely flows in and out through the stopcock valve.
  4. Close the stopcock at end-inspiration vs. end-expiration: demonstrate the concept of pleural suction evacuation (chest tube restoration).
Clinical Correlation: Teaches why chest wall penetrating trauma causes lung collapse and how subatmospheric intrapleural pressure is an absolute physiological prerequisite for negative-pressure ventilation.
